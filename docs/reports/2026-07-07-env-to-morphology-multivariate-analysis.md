# Environment -> Morphology: Multivariate Analysis

**Direction: environment -> morphology.** `docs/reports/2026-07-07-data-tier-descriptive-analysis.md`
asked "how well does a CPI image's embedding predict the environment it
was captured in?" (384 numbers -> 7 scalars, one regression per scalar).
This report asks the reverse and more genuinely multivariate question:
"how well do the 7 environmental variables (`Tair_C, P_hPa, Si, qv, Lat,
Lon, Alt_m` — no vertical-velocity variable exists in this dataset)
explain CPI image morphology, treated as a joint 384-dim response rather
than 384 independent targets?" **R² / correlation values in this report
are not comparable to that report's numbers — only comparable within this
report, across methods, on the same target.**

**Embedding source: `cls_features` (384-dim) only**, for the same reason
as the prior report's conclusion: `cls_features` was already shown to be
strictly more informative than `head_features` (64-dim) on every
env-prediction target. `head_features` is not loaded anywhere in this
script.

Full CSVs: `logs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/`. Figures:
`figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/`. Reproduce with
`python scripts/analyze_embedding_multivariate.py`.

## 1. Method

L2 (1,828,818 rows, every core variable present) joined to `cls_features`
via the same memory-safe streamed join used by `analyze_data_tiers.py`.
Six methods, increasing in flexibility:

| Step | Method | X | Y | n |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | PCA | — | `cls_features` (384-dim) | 1,828,818 |
| 2a | PLS | 7 env vars | full 384-dim | 500,000 |
| 2b | PLS | 7 env vars | top-50 PCs | 1,828,818 |
| 3a | CCA | 7 env vars | full 384-dim | 500,000 |
| 3b | CCA | 7 env vars | top-10 PCs | 1,828,818 |
| 4 | Random Forest | 7 env vars | PC1..PC10 (one RF per target) | 300,000 |
| 5 | UMAP | — | `cls_features` (384-dim) | 50,000 |
| 6 | SHAP | RF models from step 4 | — | 500/target |

All held-out splits use the same 80/20 permutation idiom (`RNG_SEED=42`)
as `analyze_data_tiers.py`'s `ols_r2`, so OLS/PLS/RF comparisons on the
same target use identical row partitions. Package versions: scikit-learn
1.9.0, umap-learn 0.5.12, shap 0.52.0 (see `run_config.json`).

**SHAP sample size**: `shap.TreeExplainer` has no fast native path for
`sklearn.RandomForestRegressor` (unlike XGBoost) and ran single-threaded.
A first attempt at 5,000 samples/target was cancelled after ~16 minutes
having completed fewer than 3 of 10 targets (linear extrapolation: 60-90
min total). Cut to 500 samples/target — the full 10-target loop then
finished in ~11 minutes, and per-target SHAP rankings still agree with
each RF's own `feature_importances_` (Spearman ρ = 0.64-0.96 across all
10 targets, median 0.86 — see §6), so the smaller sample did not
meaningfully degrade the ranking.

## 2. PCA (step 1)

Full 384-component spectrum confirms the prior report exactly: top-10 of
384 PCs explain **27.02%** of embedding variance (cross-checked bit-for-bit
against `logs/analyze_data_tiers/latest/l2_embedding_cls_pca_variance.csv`'s
0.2702). No sharp elbow — `cls_features`' variance is spread thin across
many components, consistent with a general-purpose SSL backbone
representation rather than one built for a narrow downstream task.

## 3. PLS regression (step 2)

Capped at `n_components<=7` (rank of the 7-variable env predictor set).
Held-out R² is low and rises only gradually with more components — even
using all 7 env variables jointly, they explain **~4.2%** of full-384-dim
morphology variance (2a) or **~5.1%** of top-50-PC variance (2b):

## 4. CCA (step 3)

CCA maximizes *correlation* between latent X/Y scores; PLS maximizes
*covariance* (which folds in how much variance each latent axis actually
carries). They answer different questions, and here they disagree sharply
in magnitude — expected, not a bug (see below):

![CCA canonical correlations](../../figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/cca_canonical_correlations.png)

### PLS vs CCA: what's the difference, and do they agree?

For the full-384-dim Y (2a vs 3a), CCA's first canonical correlation is
**0.98** while PLS's held-out R² across all 7 components together is only
**0.042**. This is not a contradiction: with only 7 env predictors free to
combine and 384 embedding dimensions to search, CCA can always find *some*
linear combination of those 384 dimensions that correlates almost
perfectly with *some* combination of the 7 env variables — even if that
particular direction captures a vanishingly small share of the embedding's
total variance. PLS's R² instead scores prediction of the *whole,
variance-weighted* 384-dim target, most of which (per PCA above) is
env-independent, so the average is dragged down. n=500,000 vs. 384 output
dims (ratio ~1,300:1) rules out the degenerate small-n/large-p CCA
overfitting failure mode, so the high canonical correlation is real — it
just describes a thin, high-correlation subspace, not the bulk of image
variation.

Where the two methods *are* directly comparable (3b vs 2b, both against
PCA-score targets) they agree qualitatively: both decline monotonically
from component 1 to component 7 (CCA: 0.46 -> 0.04; PLS gains taper from
+0.019 for the 2nd component to +0.010 for the 7th), i.e. both show
component 1 carrying the most shared signal and rapidly diminishing
returns after. **Conclusion: environment explains a small, real,
consistent slice of morphology overall — CCA's headline number should not
be read as "most of image variation is environment-driven."**

One RF (`n_estimators=300, max_depth=16, min_samples_leaf=5`) per PC1-10
target, 300,000-row sample, compared to a linear OLS baseline on

0.055, i.e. roughly 3x on average — nonlinear structure genuinely helps):

| PC8 | 0.112 | 0.185 |

Feature importance, averaged across all 10 targets: **Lat (0.27) > Lon
(0.16) > qv (0.13) > Alt_m (0.13) > Tair_C (0.11) > P_hPa (0.10) > Si
(0.09)**.

![RF feature importance](../../figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/rf_feature_importance_bars.png)

**Caveat**: Lat/Lon's dominance is very likely largely a *campaign*
proxy rather than a direct physical driver of crystal habit — different
campaigns fly in different regions (see the UMAP campaign clusters below,
which are almost fully separable), so Lat/Lon can substitute for "which
campaign" almost one-to-one. This mirrors the pooled-vs-within-campaign
distinction the prior report used to isolate genuine physical
relationships; this analysis did not repeat that split (out of scope
here), so the RF/SHAP rankings should be read as pooled, confound-inclusive
results, not evidence that geographic position itself shapes ice crystals.

## 6. UMAP (step 5)

50,000-point sample, same `COLORS`/`STYLE` as the prior report's PCA
plots for visual comparability.

![UMAP by campaign](../../figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/umap_by_campaign.png)

UMAP shows **dramatically more structure than linear PCA** — campaigns
form nearly-separable clusters here, versus a single overlapping blob in
`figs/analyze_data_tiers/latest/l2_embedding_cls_pca_by_campaign.png`
(PC1 vs PC2). This confirms the embedding *does* encode strong
campaign-level structure; PCA's top-2 axes just aren't where it lives.

![UMAP by env](../../figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/umap_by_env.png)

Colored by environmental variable, `Tair_C`, `Alt_m`, and `P_hPa` all show
clear, smooth gradients that track the campaign clusters (e.g. the ATTREX
cluster, a stratospheric platform, sits at the extreme cold/high-altitude
end). **`Si` shows no visible spatial gradient** — consistent with every
other method in both this report and the prior one finding Si the
weakest-encoded variable, now confirmed from the reverse direction too.

## 7. SHAP (step 6)

`TreeExplainer` (exact, no background dataset needed for
`feature_perturbation="tree_path_dependent"`), run on 500 held-out rows
per target (see Method for why), plotted for the 3 highest-R² targets
(PC3, PC4, PC8):

![SHAP PC3](../../figs/analyze_embedding_multivariate/latest/shap_summary_PC3.png)

Ranking matches RF's `feature_importances_` closely: `Lat`, `Tair_C`, and
`Alt_m` are consistently the top 3 by mean |SHAP value| for PC3 (as they
are by RF importance), with `Si` again near the bottom. Spearman
correlation between SHAP ranking and RF importance is positive for every
one of the 10 targets (ρ = 0.64-0.96, median 0.86) — no target shows the
kind of disagreement that would flag a bug rather than genuine agreement
between the two methods.

## 8. Interpretation

- **Environment explains a real but modest, consistently small slice of
  CPI image morphology** — this holds across every method that reports a
  variance-explained quantity (PLS: ~4-5%; RF: 9-33% per top-10 PC,
  averaging ~16%), all far below the *reverse*-direction R² values in the
  prior report (embedding -> environment reached 0.23-0.96 depending on
  variable). Morphology is a much richer, higher-dimensional signal than
  any combination of 7 scalar env readings can fully account for — expected,
  since crystal habit depends on growth history along a trajectory, not
  just the instantaneous state where it was imaged.
- **Si is the weakest env variable in this direction too** — lowest mean
  RF importance (0.094, vs. next-lowest P_hPa at 0.101) and no visible
  UMAP gradient — mirroring the prior report's finding that Si is the
  faintest signal in the embedding -> environment direction as well.
  Combined, this is a fairly strong cross-method, cross-direction signal
  that ice supersaturation's imprint on (or from) CPI imagery is real but
  consistently the hardest of the 7 variables to recover.
- **Nonlinear methods add real value here**: RF beats linear OLS by ~3x on
  average, and UMAP reveals almost-separable campaign clustering that's
  invisible in 2D linear PCA — env/morphology relationships in this
  direction are not well captured by linear methods alone.
- **CCA's high canonical correlations are a genuine but narrow finding**,
  not evidence that environment dominates morphology (see §4) — the
  broader-coverage methods (PLS, RF) tell the real-magnitude story.
- **Lat/Lon dominance likely reflects campaign identity, not a direct
  physical mechanism** (see §5 caveat) — a natural follow-up would be a
  within-campaign version of the RF/SHAP step, as the prior report did for
  its regressions, to isolate genuine physical drivers from geography.

## 9. Caveats

- Pooled across campaigns throughout (RF, PLS, CCA, UMAP) — cross-campaign
  confounds (instrument, region, season) are not separated out here, per
  the Lat/Lon caveat in §5.
- SHAP uses 500 rows/target for runtime reasons (see Method); rankings
  are stable per the RF-agreement check, but exact mean-|SHAP| magnitudes
  would sharpen with a larger sample if this needs revisiting later.
- PLS/CCA full-Y variants (2a/3a) subsample to 500,000 of 1,828,818 rows
  for memory; the top-K-PC variants (2b/3b) use the full row count.
- No vertical-velocity variable exists in this dataset's schema, so it's
  absent from the 7 env predictors throughout (per the original ChatGPT
  recommendation this analysis was based on).
